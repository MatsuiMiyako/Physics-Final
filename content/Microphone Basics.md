## What does a microphone do?
There are many different types of microphones but all of the microphones share the basic function of converting [[sound waves]] into [[electrical energy]]. To reiterate, the basic function of a microphone is to detect changes in air pressure and changing that into electricity. The electrical signal is converted from when the sound waves hit the microphone's [[diaphragm]].

## Differences Between Microphone Types
### [[Dynamic Microphone]]
- Uses [[electromagnetic induction]]
- A diaphragm is attached to a coil of wire
- The coil moves inside a [[magnetic field]]
- Movement creates a [[voltage]]
Key traits:
- No external power needed
- Very durable
- Handles loud sounds well
- Lower sensitivity
Common use: Live vocals, drums

### [[Condenser Microphone]]
- Uses a [[capacitor]]
- Diaphragm + [[backplate]] form two [[charged]] plates
- Sound changes the distance between plates
- This changes [[capacitance]] to create signals
Key traits:
- Needs power ([[phantom power]])
- Very sensitive
- Captures detail well
- More fragile
**Common use:** Studio recording, vocals, acoustic instruments

### [[Electret Microphone]]
- A special type of condenser mic
- Has a permanently [[Charged|charged]] material ([[electret]])
- Does not need external voltage to [[Electrical Charge|charge]] the [[Microphone Capsule|capsule]]
- Still needs power for internal electronics ([[FET]])
Key traits:
- Small
- Cheap
- Low power
- Common in phones, laptops, headsets
Common use: Embedded electronics
